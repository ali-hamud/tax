  Important Notice for US Citizens and Green Card Holders
US Citizens and Long-Term Green Card Holders Subject to Expatriation Taxation
We want to take this opportunity to remind you of the US expatriation tax rules and discuss how you may be affected.
The US tax law applies an expatriation tax to US citizens who relinquish their citizenship and also to certain long-term residents who voluntarily or involuntarily lose their green card or elect to be taxed as a resident of another country under an income tax treaty of the US. A long-term resident is a non-US citizen who has held a US permanent resident visa (or green card) in eight out of the last 15 years. A holder of a green card for as little as six years and two days can meet this test (i.e., one day in the first year, full year for the following six years and one day in the last year).
US citizens and long-term residents are subject to the expatriation tax if they meet any of the following conditions:
  • their average annual net income tax liability for the five years prior to expatriation is in excess of US$201,000 for individuals expatriating in 2024 (this figure is adjusted annually); or
    		•
  • their net worth is US$2 million or more; or
    		•
  • they fail to certify that they are in compliance with all US tax obligations for the preceding five years.
    		•
    US citizens and long-term residents who expatriated before 17 Jun 2008 continue to be subject to the prior expatriation tax rules for 10 years subsequent to expatriation. Individuals who expatriate on or after 17 Jun 2008 are subject to a 'mark-to-market' rule whereby they are treated as having sold their worldwide assets for their fair market value on the day before expatriation. All resulting gain in excess of US$866,000 is subject to tax. Special rules apply to certain deferred compensation, tax deferred accounts and non-grantor trusts. In addition, gifts or bequests to US citizens and residents from individuals subject to these rules can give rise to tax in the hands of recipients.
    The information provided above is general in nature. All individuals holding Green Cards or considering applying for one should carefully consider the expatriation tax. Careful planning is required to identify and control its impact. If you have any questions regarding the expatriation tax, please contact your KPMG tax representative.
